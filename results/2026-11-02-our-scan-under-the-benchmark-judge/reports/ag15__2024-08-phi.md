# Audit report

## [High] Unprotected public _addCredIdPerAddress/_removeCredIdPerAddress let anyone corrupt curator lists and lock shares

File: Cred.sol
Function: _removeCredIdPerAddress() / _addCredIdPerAddress()

The two helpers that maintain _credIdsPerAddress, _credIdsPerAddressCredIdIndex and _credIdsPerAddressArrLength are declared public with no access control, although they are meant to be internal bookkeeping called only from _updateCuratorShareBalance. Anyone can call _removeCredIdPerAddress(credId, victim) to pop a victim's credId from his array, or _addCredIdPerAddress(credId, victim) to push duplicates and overwrite the stored index. When the victim later sells his last shares, _updateCuratorShareBalance calls _removeCredIdPerAddress, which reverts with EmptyArray, IndexOutofBounds or WrongCredId because the bookkeeping no longer matches. The sale (single or batch) therefore reverts.

Attack path: 1) Victim buys shares of credId X. 2) Attacker calls Cred._removeCredIdPerAddress(X, victim) (or pushes a different credId to shift the index). 3) Victim calls sellShareCred(X, fullBalance, 0); _updateCuratorShareBalance hits currentNum-amount==0 and calls _removeCredIdPerAddress, which reverts.

Impact: Victims can never fully exit a position; ETH locked in the bonding curve for their last shares (they can only sell all-but-one). getPositionsForCurator data is also corrupted.

## [High] createArt signature does not bind artist/receiver/config, has no nonce or chainId: art creation can be hijacked

File: PhiFactory.sol
Function: createArt()

_validateArtCreationSignature only verifies the phiSigner signature over signedData_ = (expiresIn, uri, credData). The CreateConfig (artist, receiver, maxSupply, mintFee, startTime, endTime, soulBounded) is passed separately and is not covered by the signature, there is no nonce or used-signature mapping, and no chainId or contract address in the signed payload. Any observer of a pending createArt transaction can copy signedData_ and signature_ and submit it with his own CreateConfig, becoming artist (control over updateArtSettings and royalties) and receiver of all mint fees. The same signature can also be reused unlimited times before expiry and across chains where the factory is deployed.

Attack path: 1) Legit artist submits createArt(sig, data, config). 2) Attacker front-runs (or later replays) with the same sig and data but config.artist = config.receiver = attacker. 3) Attacker's art is created for the cred, collects artist rewards on every mint and controls settings.

Impact: Theft of art ownership and all artist mint rewards; unlimited unauthorized art creation per signature, cross-chain replay.

## [High] claim() forwards mintFee from the factory's own balance regardless of msg.value

File: PhiFactory.sol
Function: claim() / batchClaim()

claim() computes mintFee = getArtMintFee(artId, quantity_) and calls this.merkleClaim{value: mintFee} or this.signatureClaim{value: mintFee} without checking that msg.value >= mintFee. The value check in _validateAndUpdateClaimState is done against msg.value of the inner self-call, which the factory itself funds. Any ETH sitting in the factory is therefore spent on behalf of a caller who paid nothing. The factory does accumulate user ETH: overpayment in claim() (msg.value - mintFee) is never refunded, and in batchClaim the per-item excess ethValue_[i] - mintFee stays in the contract as well.

Attack path: 1) Users overpay through claim/batchClaim, leaving ETH in PhiFactory. 2) Attacker creates (or uses) an art where he is receiver, with a high mintFee, and calls claim() with msg.value = 0 and a valid merkle/signature claim. 3) Factory pays the full mintFee from its balance; the art reward is credited to the attacker's PhiRewards balance and withdrawn.

Impact: Theft of all ETH held by PhiFactory (users' overpayments); users who overpay lose their excess.

## [High] Share lock bypass via refund callback in buy, enabling atomic buy-distribute-sell of curator rewards

File: Cred.sol
Function: _handleTrade()

_handleTrade has no nonReentrant guard. On a buy it updates share balance and currentSupply, then sends excessPayment to _msgSender() via safeTransferETH, and only afterwards sets lastTradeTimestamp[credId][curator]. During the refund callback lastTradeTimestamp is still the old value (0 for a fresh address), so sellShareCred passes the SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD check. An attacker contract can thus hold a large position for zero time inside one transaction. Because CuratorRewardsDistributor.distribute pays out according to current share balances, the attacker can call distribute inside the callback and capture most of the accumulated curator rewards.

Attack path: 1) Attacker contract calls buyShareCred(X, N, 0) with msg.value > cost. 2) In receive(), it calls CuratorRewardsDistributor.distribute(X) (receiving the 1% caller fee plus a pro-rata share of rewards for N shares). 3) Still in receive(), calls sellShareCred(X, N, 0); lock check passes because lastTradeTimestamp is not yet updated. 4) Outer buy finishes.

Impact: Curator rewards for a cred are stolen by a flash position; the 10-minute lock that is meant to prevent this is ineffective. Cost is only buy/sell fees.

## [Medium] Curator rewards distributed by spot share balance: just-in-time buyers capture rewards accrued by long-term holders

File: CuratorRewardsDistributor.sol
Function: distribute()

distribute() is permissionless and splits the entire balanceOf[credId] (accumulated from many art mints over time) by credContract.getShareNumber at the moment of the call. There is no time-weighting or snapshot. Anyone can buy a large number of shares, call distribute in the same block, and sell after SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD (10 minutes). Curators who held while rewards accrued but are diluted at distribution time lose most of their share.

Attack path: 1) Rewards accumulate in balanceOf[X]. 2) Attacker buys many shares of X. 3) Attacker calls distribute(X) and gets the 1% caller fee plus a proportional cut. 4) After 10 minutes attacker sells.

Impact: Systematic dilution/theft of curator rewards from genuine holders.

## [Medium] buyShareCredFor lets anyone reset a victim's share lock, preventing sales indefinitely

File: Cred.sol
Function: buyShareCredFor()

buyShareCredFor(credId, amount, curator_, maxPrice) lets any caller buy shares for an arbitrary curator_, and _handleTrade then sets lastTradeTimestamp[credId_][curator_] = block.timestamp. Sales by that curator revert with ShareLockPeriodNotPassed for the next 10 minutes. The same happens through batchBuyShareCred with an arbitrary curator_. An attacker who buys 1 share for the victim every 10 minutes keeps the victim from selling, for example while the price falls or ahead of a royalty change.

Attack path: 1) Victim wants to sell shares of X. 2) Attacker calls buyShareCredFor(X, 1, victim, 0) (or front-runs the victim's sell tx). 3) Victim's sellShareCred reverts; repeat.

Impact: Victims cannot exit positions when they want; price-sensitive loss.

## [Medium] Excess ETH paid through PhiNFT1155 merkleClaim/signatureClaim is refunded to the NFT contract and locked

File: PhiFactory.sol
Function: _processClaim()

Claimable.signatureClaim and merkleClaim on the NFT contract forward the full msg.value to PhiFactory. In _processClaim the refund of etherValue_ - mintFee is sent to _msgSender(), which is the PhiNFT1155 contract, not the user. PhiNFT1155 accepts it via receive() but has no function to withdraw ETH. The user's overpayment is therefore permanently stuck in the art contract.

Attack path: 1) User calls PhiNFT1155.merkleClaim with msg.value larger than the mint fee (for instance because fees changed). 2) Factory refunds the difference to the NFT contract. 3) No way to recover it.

Impact: Permanent loss of user overpayment.

## [Medium] Shares holders map never removes zero-balance curators: distribute() loops can be made to run out of gas

File: Cred.sol
Function: _updateCuratorShareBalance()

When a curator sells all shares, _updateCuratorShareBalance calls shareBalance[credId_].set(sender_, 0) instead of remove, so the EnumerableMap keeps every address that ever held shares. _getCuratorData iterates over shareBalance[credId_].length(), and CuratorRewardsDistributor.distribute then calls getShareNumber twice per holder. An attacker can cheaply inflate the map by buying and selling one share from many addresses (MAX_SUPPLY bounds only concurrent supply, not past holders). Once the loops exceed the block gas limit, distribute reverts forever.

Attack path: 1) Attacker repeatedly buys 1 share of X from fresh addresses and sells after the lock. 2) shareBalance[X] grows unbounded. 3) distribute(X) runs out of gas.

Impact: Curator rewards in CuratorRewardsDistributor for that cred are permanently stuck.

## [Medium] Creator can change buy/sell royalty to 50% at any time and front-run curators' sells

File: Cred.sol
Function: updateCred()

updateCred lets the cred creator set buyShareRoyalty_ and sellShareRoyalty_ up to MAX_ROYALTY_RANGE = 5000 (50%) at any time; the royalty values are not part of the signed data and take effect immediately. getPriceData reads the current royalty at trade time. Sellers using minPrice_ = 0 (the default in createCred and common in frontends) or batch sells can be front-run by the creator raising the sell royalty, taking up to half of the sale proceeds. Curators who bought under low royalties have no protection or delay.

Attack path: 1) Curators buy under 0% sell royalty. 2) Creator sees a large sell and calls updateCred with sellShareRoyalty = 5000. 3) The sell executes paying 50% to the creator via PhiRewards.

Impact: Up to 50% of curator sale proceeds can be taken by the creator.

## [Medium] Factory signatureClaim ignores the signed chainId: signatures replayable across chains

File: PhiFactory.sol
Function: signatureClaim()

encodeData_ is decoded as (expiresIn, minter, ref, verifier, artId, chainId, data) but the chainId field is skipped and never compared with block.chainid. The signature is checked over keccak256(encodeData_) and only binds the contents, not the executing chain. Anyone can call PhiFactory.signatureClaim directly on another chain with an encodeData_ signed for chain A. Because artIds are sequential per deployment, the same artId on chain B can refer to a different, gated art. There is also no used-signature tracking, so a signature can be used repeatedly before expiry.

Attack path: 1) Obtain a valid signature for minter M, artId N on chain A. 2) Call PhiFactory.signatureClaim(sig, sameEncodeData, mintArgs) on chain B. 3) Mint of art N on chain B succeeds without eligibility there.

Impact: Mints of signature-gated arts by ineligible users; verifier/referral rewards misdirected.

## [Medium] createArt reverts when msg.value exceeds artCreateFee because refund goes to PhiFactory which cannot receive ETH

File: PhiNFT1155.sol
Function: createArtFromFactory()

createArtFromFactory refunds msg.value - artFee to _msgSender(), which is PhiFactory. PhiFactory has no receive or fallback function, so SafeTransferLib.safeTransferETH reverts, the low-level call returns false, and createArt reverts with CreateFailed. Any overpayment, including a race with the owner changing artCreateFee via setArtCreatFee, makes art creation fail. Even if the factory could receive, the refund would never reach the artist.

Attack path: 1) Artist calls createArt with msg.value > artCreateFee (or the owner lowers the fee just before). 2) Refund to the factory reverts. 3) createArt reverts.

Impact: Art creation DoS unless the exact fee is sent; overpayment cannot be refunded to the user.

## [Medium] PhiNFT1155 caches protocolFeeDestination at initialize; factory updates never propagate

File: PhiNFT1155.sol
Function: createArtFromFactory()

PhiNFT1155.initialize stores protocolFeeDestination = phiFactoryContract.protocolFeeDestination() once, and createArtFromFactory always pays artFee to this cached address. PhiFactory.setProtocolFeeDestination only updates the factory's own variable, and PhiNFT1155 has no setter. After a destination change (for example a compromised or rotated treasury), every existing art contract keeps sending art creation fees to the old address. The default ERC2981 royalty recipient set in initializeRoyalties is likewise frozen.

Attack path: 1) Owner rotates protocolFeeDestination on the factory. 2) New arts on existing cred NFT contracts still pay artCreateFee to the old address.

Impact: Protocol fees lost or sent to a compromised address.

## [Medium] createCred/updateCred signatures skip the chainId field and have no nonce, allowing replay

File: Cred.sol
Function: createCred() / updateCred()

Both functions decode a third uint256 field from signedData_ (presumably chainId) but discard it, and there is no nonce or used-signature mapping. A signer-approved createCred payload can therefore be reused by the same sender to create any number of creds, including MERKLE creds with the signed merkle root, and on every chain where Cred is deployed, until expiresIn. An updateCred payload can be replayed later to revert credURL to an older value before expiry.

Attack path: 1) Sender obtains one signed createCred payload. 2) Calls createCred repeatedly or on another chain with the same signature.

Impact: Bypass of the phi signer's per-cred approval: unauthorized creds with arbitrary creator/royalties.

## [High] Public _addCredIdPerAddress/_removeCredIdPerAddress let anyone corrupt a holder's cred index and block their final sell

File: Cred.sol
Function: _removeCredIdPerAddress()/_addCredIdPerAddress()

Both bookkeeping helpers are declared public with no access check, although only _updateCuratorShareBalance is meant to call them (they are not used anywhere else in src, test or script). _removeCredIdPerAddress(credId, victim) validates only that the stored index points at credId, then swaps and pops the victim's entry and deletes _credIdsPerAddressCredIdIndex[victim][credId]. _credIdExistsPerAddress[victim][credId] stays true and shareBalance is untouched, so the two structures diverge. When the victim later sells down to zero, _updateCuratorShareBalance calls _removeCredIdPerAddress again, which now reads index 0 (the default after the delete) and reverts with EmptyArray or WrongCredId (or IndexOutofBounds). _addCredIdPerAddress(credId, victim) lets anyone push duplicate or stale ids and rewrite the index mapping for the victim, which corrupts getPositionsForCurator output.

Attack path: 1) Victim holds shares of cred X (array contains X). 2) Attacker calls _removeCredIdPerAddress(X, victim) for the price of gas. 3) Victim calls sellShareCred or batchSellShareCred for their entire balance of X: shareBalance would reach 0, so _removeCredIdPerAddress(X, victim) runs and reverts, rolling back the whole sell (and the whole batch). 4) Victim can still sell partially, leaving at least one share. 5) The victim can repair only by first calling the same public _addCredIdPerAddress(X, victim), and the attacker can remove again before the sell lands.

Impact: Any account can cheaply block any holder from exiting their last shares of a cred (and make batch sells revert), and can corrupt the per-address position list. The exit is front-runnable and the repair is non-obvious, so funds can stay locked as long as the attacker keeps griefing.

## [Medium] Buy refund is sent before lastTradeTimestamp is written, so reentrancy bypasses the 10-minute share lock

File: Cred.sol
Function: _handleTrade()

On a buy, _handleTrade updates shareBalance and currentSupply, then sends the excess payment to _msgSender() with safeTransferETH, and only after that writes lastTradeTimestamp[credId_][curator_] = block.timestamp. The single-trade path has no nonReentrant (the modifier is only applied to _executeBatchTrade, and locked is not held here). During the refund callback the sell check block.timestamp <= lastTradeTimestamp + SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD still reads the old (or zero) timestamp, so a sell of the just-bought shares passes. The lock that is meant to stop flash in-and-out trading therefore does not work for a contract buyer.

Attack path: 1) Attacker contract (with flash-loaned ETH) calls buyShareCred(credId, n, 0) sending cost plus 1 wei. 2) State is updated and the 1 wei refund hits the attacker's receive(). 3) Inside the callback it calls CuratorRewardsDistributor.distribute(credId); rewards are split by current share balances, so the freshly bought n shares take a large part of the accumulated balanceOf[credId] pot (and the caller also gets the 1 percent royalty). 4) Still in the callback it calls sellShareCred(credId, n, 0); the lock passes. 5) The flash loan is repaid and the attacker withdraws the credited rewards from PhiRewards.

Impact: Curator rewards accumulated by long-term holders can be captured by a one-transaction, no-holding-risk buyer who only pays round-trip protocol and royalty fees. The same bypass defeats the SHARE_LOCK_PERIOD for any other mechanism that relies on it, and it also lets one attacker create many zero-balance holder entries in one transaction (see the distribute DoS finding).

## [Medium] buyShareCredFor and batchBuyShareCred let anyone reset a third party's share lock indefinitely

File: Cred.sol
Function: buyShareCredFor()/_executeBatchTrade()

buyShareCredFor and batchBuyShareCred accept an arbitrary curator_ and write lastTradeTimestamp[credId][curator_] = block.timestamp for that address (in _handleTrade and in _executeBatchTrade). Selling requires block.timestamp > lastTradeTimestamp[credId][curator] + 10 minutes, and the lock applies to the whole position of the curator, not only to the newly gifted share. Nothing requires the curator to consent. The payer simply donates one share, so the attacker's cost per reset is one share price plus fees, and a cred creator even gets the creator royalty back.

Attack path: 1) Victim holds shares of cred X and wants to sell. 2) Attacker calls buyShareCredFor(X, 1, victim, 0) (or a batch with the victim as curator_), paying about one share price. 3) The victim's lastTradeTimestamp is now the current time, so sellShareCred and batchSellShareCred revert with ShareLockPeriodNotPassed. 4) The attacker repeats this every few minutes, keeping the victim locked, for example during a price drop or while the creator rugs through royalties.

Impact: Holders can be prevented from selling for as long as the attacker is willing to keep paying a small amount per 10 minutes, which can be used for targeted market manipulation or to trap exits at a chosen time.

## [Medium] Zero-balance holders are never removed from the curator map, so distribute() can be bricked by gas and the pot stuck

File: CuratorRewardsDistributor.sol
Function: distribute()

On a sell, _updateCuratorShareBalance calls shareBalance[credId_].set(sender_, currentNum - amount_), which writes 0 but never removes the key, so the map length equals every address that has ever held a share. This is not bounded by MAX_SUPPLY (999) because that only caps the live supply, not the number of historic holders. distribute() calls getCuratorAddresses(credId, 0, 0), which iterates the full map (at() plus a tryGet per entry), then runs two more loops with an external getShareNumber call per address, then depositBatch writes a storage balance per address. There is no paginated or sweep alternative, and balanceOf[credId] can only leave the contract through distribute. This is a liveness bug, different from the known issue about OZ EnumerableMap gas inefficiency.

Attack path: 1) Attacker (or a script contract deploying helper contracts, using the lock bypass above or waiting 10 minutes) makes many fresh addresses each buy one share and sell it again, leaving zero-balance entries; buyShareCredFor(credId, 1, freshAddr) is another way to add holders. 2) Each stale entry adds several thousand gas to every distribute() call. 3) Once the total exceeds the block gas limit, distribute(credId) always runs out of gas. 4) The balanceOf[credId] curator pot (fed by 0.00005 ETH per mint) and all future deposits for that cred are permanently undistributable.

Impact: Curator rewards of a targeted cred can be locked forever for all holders at low cost on a cheap-gas L2. Even without an attacker, a cred with several hundred real holders can approach the limit.

## [Medium] createArt signature is not bound to the caller or to createConfig_, so anyone can reuse it with their own artist and receiver

File: PhiFactory.sol
Function: createArt()

_validateArtCreationSignature only verifies a Phi signature over signedData_ = (expiresIn, uri, credData). The struct createConfig_ (artist, receiver, maxSupply, mintFee, start and end time, soulBounded) is passed separately and is never signed, msg.sender is not part of the signed data, there is no nonce, and the signature is not consumed. Any observer of a pending or mined createArt transaction can therefore call createArt with the same signedData_ and signature_ but their own createConfig_ until expiresIn passes. The new art gets artist = attacker, so onlyArtCreator in updateArtSettings and in PhiNFT1155.updateRoyalties belongs to the attacker, and the attacker is the receiver of the artist rewards of that art.

Attack path: 1) Honest artist obtains a signature for a cred and submits createArt. 2) Attacker copies signedData_ and signature_ from the mempool or calldata and calls createArt with createConfig_ naming themselves as artist and receiver. 3) A Phi-signed uri and cred metadata now sit behind an art controlled by the attacker, who can set mintFee, receiver and royalties, and a single signature can mint any number of arts for that cred. 4) credMerkleRoot[credChainId][credId] is also rewritten on each such call.

Impact: Art squatting and impersonation of a verified cred, with mint fees and artist rewards of that art going to the attacker. This also lets one signature be used for unlimited art creations until expiry.

## [Medium] PhiFactory.claim never checks msg.value against the mint fee and pays from the contract's own balance, stranding user overpayments

File: PhiFactory.sol
Function: claim()/batchClaim()

claim() computes mintFee = getArtMintFee(artId, quantity_) and forwards exactly that amount with this.merkleClaim{value: mintFee} or this.signatureClaim{value: mintFee}. It never compares msg.value to mintFee. The inner call sees msg.value == mintFee, so the refund branch in _processClaim sends nothing, and any surplus sent to claim() (or left over from the per-item ethValue_[i] in batchClaim) stays in the factory. Conversely, if msg.value is less than mintFee, the shortfall is paid out of whatever ETH the factory already holds, including other users' stranded surplus. The only way out is the owner-only withdraw(), which sweeps to protocolFeeDestination.

Attack path: 1) Users overpay claim() or batchClaim (for example because the price changed between quote and execution, or ethValue_ entries are unevenly split); the surplus accumulates in the factory. 2) Anyone calls claim() or batchClaim with msg.value 0 (batchClaim lets the caller choose ethValue_[i], including zeros, as long as the sum equals msg.value). 3) The factory pays the mint fee from the accumulated balance and the free mint succeeds.

Impact: Overpayments are lost to users and can be drained by later free claims; the amounts per claim are small but unbounded in total, and the invariant that each claimant pays the full mint fee is not enforced in this entry path.

## [Medium] Unsigned imageURI_ lets a third party overwrite a minter's per-minter token URI by claiming for them

File: PhiNFT1155.sol
Function: mint()/claimFromFactory()

imageURI_ (and quantity_) are neither part of the Merkle leaf (keccak(minter_, leafPart_)) nor of the signed claimData (expiresIn, minter, ref, verifier, artId, chainid, data), and the caller does not have to be the minter. mint() then writes advancedTokenURI[tokenId_][to_] = imageURI_ unconditionally on every claim, and uri(tokenId, minter) returns it. For MERKLE arts, anyone who knows the public tree data can call claim, merkleClaim or the NFT's merkleClaim for any eligible minter X with any imageURI_ and quantity. For SIGNATURE arts the same works with a replayed, still valid signature of X (replay is a known issue, the unsigned field is the new part).

Attack path: 1) Minter X is eligible (Merkle leaf or Phi signature). 2) Attacker calls factory.claim or merkleClaim for minter_ = X with an attacker-chosen imageURI_ (for example a phishing or offensive image) and any quantity, paying only the mint fee. 3) advancedTokenURI[tokenId][X] now holds the attacker's URI, and X receives unsolicited tokens (and an ERC1155 receiver callback if X is a contract). 4) X can only fix it by claiming again themselves and paying the fee.

Impact: Third parties can alter how a victim's credential NFT is displayed and force-mint to them, which undermines the integrity of the per-minter metadata.

## [Medium] updateCred sets buy and sell royalties from unsigned arguments, so a creator can raise them to 50 percent right before trades

File: Cred.sol
Function: updateCred()

updateCred verifies a Phi signature over (expiresIn, sender, chainId, credId, credURL), but buyShareRoyalty_ and sellShareRoyalty_ are plain function arguments outside the signed data and are only capped by MAX_ROYALTY_RANGE (5000 bps = 50 percent). Any creator who holds one unexpired updateCred signature can change the royalties at any time before expiry, and the signature is reusable. _handleTrade reads the current royalty at execution time and the slippage limit is skipped when maxPrice_ or minPrice_ is 0.

Attack path: 1) Creator obtains a signature for a normal URL update. 2) A large holder submits sellShareCred (or a buyer submits buyShareCred) with limit 0. 3) Creator front-runs with updateCred setting sellShareRoyalty (or buyShareRoyalty) to 5000. 4) The trade executes with up to half of the proceeds (or of the price on top of it) routed to the creator via PhiRewards.deposit.

Impact: Holders and buyers can lose up to about half of a trade to a creator that changes fees on the fly; users who set a price limit are protected, those who use 0 are not.

## [Medium] Excess ETH sent through PhiNFT1155 signatureClaim/merkleClaim is refunded to the NFT contract and stuck

File: Claimable.sol
Function: signatureClaim()/merkleClaim()

The NFT contract's signatureClaim() and merkleClaim() forward the whole msg.value to the factory. In PhiFactory._processClaim the surplus (etherValue_ - mintFee) is refunded to _msgSender(), which in this path is the PhiNFT1155 clone. The clone accepts it through its empty receive(), but has no function to pay it out to the user or anyone else (createArtFromFactory only spends msg.value, not the contract balance). The user who overpaid therefore loses the surplus permanently.

Attack path: 1) User calls nft.signatureClaim() or nft.merkleClaim() with msg.value above getArtMintFee (the exact fee is easy to misquote when rewards or fees change). 2) The factory refunds the difference to the NFT contract. 3) The ETH stays in the clone forever.

Impact: Permanent loss of user overpayments, and ETH accumulation in every art contract without any way to recover it.

## [Medium] PhiFactory.signatureClaim ignores the signed chain id and the art's verification type, enabling cross-chain replay

File: PhiFactory.sol
Function: signatureClaim()

signatureClaim decodes encodeData_ as (expiresIn_, minter_, ref_, verifier_, artId_, , data_) and discards the sixth field (the chain id), then only checks the Phi signature and expiry. The chain id is injected correctly by claim() and by Claimable, but the external signatureClaim accepts caller-supplied encodeData_, so a signature produced for artId N on one chain also verifies on another chain's factory that uses the same phiSignerAddress. It also does not check that arts[artId_].verificationType is SIGNATURE. Art ids are per-chain counters, so id N on the second chain refers to a different art.

Attack path: 1) A user is verified and receives a signature S for artId N on chain A (encodeData_ contains chainid A). 2) Before expiry the user calls signatureClaim(S, encodeData_, mintArgs) directly on the factory on chain B where the same signer is configured. 3) The signature validates and the user mints art N on chain B without Phi ever approving them for that art.

Impact: A verified user can mint unrelated arts on other deployments (Base, Optimism, Berachain are all planned) within the signature lifetime, bypassing the verification for those arts.

## [Medium] Cred.createCred and updateCred decode but never check the signed chain id

File: Cred.sol
Function: createCred()/updateCred()

The signed data of both functions contains a chain id slot (the tests put 31337 there) but createCred decodes it into an unnamed variable and updateCred skips it, so it is never compared with block.chainid. There is also no nonce. A signature issued for sender S on one chain therefore validates on every other chain where Cred is deployed with the same phiSignerAddress, provided the bondingCurve address in the data is whitelisted there too (updateCred needs no such condition, only that S is the creator of the same credId).

Attack path: 1) Creator S gets a signature on chain A to create or update a cred. 2) S submits the same calldata to the Cred proxy on chain B. 3) The signature check passes and the cred is created or updated on chain B without the backend ever approving it there.

Impact: Cross-chain replay of Phi approvals for cred creation and URL or royalty updates; bounded by the sender binding and the whitelist condition, so the damage is limited to unapproved creds or stale updates.
