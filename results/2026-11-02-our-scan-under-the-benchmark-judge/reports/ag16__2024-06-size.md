# Audit report

## [High] Liquidator reward mixes borrow-token units with collateral-token units, making liquidation reward negligible

File: Liquidate.sol
Function: executeLiquidate()

liquidatorReward = min(assignedCollateral - debtInCollateralToken, mulDivUp(debtPosition.futureValue, liquidationRewardPercent, PERCENT)). assignedCollateral and debtInCollateralToken are amounts of collateral token (e.g. WETH, 18 decimals), but debtPosition.futureValue is an amount of borrow token (e.g. USDC, 6 decimals) and is never converted via debtTokenAmountToCollateralTokenAmount. For a 1000 USDC debt at 5% the reward is 50e6 wei of WETH instead of the value of 50 USDC in WETH. The liquidator therefore only receives about the debt value back plus dust.

Attack path: 1) A borrower becomes underwater or overdue. 2) A liquidator calls liquidate(); it pays futureValue in borrowAToken and receives debtInCollateralToken + a dust reward. 3) After gas and price risk the liquidation is unprofitable, so rational liquidators do not act and positions remain unliquidated until bad debt.

Impact: Liquidations are not incentivised; underwater positions accumulate and lenders suffer losses / protocol insolvency. Because selfLiquidate is only allowed below 100% CR, loans between 100% and crLiquidation depend entirely on third-party liquidators, who have no reward.

## [Medium] Multicall cap check reads Size's own borrowAToken balance instead of total supply, bypassing borrowATokenCap

File: Multicall.sol
Function: multicall()

multicall sets isMulticall = true, which makes executeDeposit skip validateBorrowATokenCap. The replacement check uses borrowATokenSupplyBefore/After = state.data.borrowAToken.balanceOf(address(this)), i.e. only the balance held by the Size contract (repayments awaiting claims), not borrowAToken.totalSupply(). A deposit mints to params.to, so this value does not change and is normally far below borrowATokenCap, so validateBorrowATokenIncreaseLteDebtTokenDecrease returns early.

Attack path: 1) Supply is at borrowATokenCap; a direct deposit() reverts. 2) User calls multicall([deposit(USDC, hugeAmount, user)]). 3) Deposit skips the cap check; the final check compares Size's own balance (unchanged) with the cap and passes.

Impact: Anyone can deposit unlimited borrow tokens beyond the risk cap set by governance, defeating the cap meant to limit protocol exposure during launch.

## [Medium] Variable pool liquidity check reads underlying balance of the Aave Pool instead of the aToken

File: CapsLibrary.sol
Function: validateVariablePoolHasEnoughLiquidity()

liquidity = underlyingBorrowToken.balanceOf(address(variablePool)). In Aave v3 the underlying reserves are held by the aToken contract (reserveData.aTokenAddress), the Pool contract itself holds no underlying. The returned liquidity is therefore ~0 (or arbitrary dust) and the check either always reverts or does not measure real withdrawable liquidity. It is called after every buyCreditMarket, sellCreditMarket and liquidateWithReplacement.

Attack path: 1) Protocol is deployed on real Aave v3. 2) Any user calls sellCreditMarket / buyCreditMarket with amount > 0. 3) validateVariablePoolHasEnoughLiquidity compares amount to the Pool's underlying balance (0) and reverts with NOT_ENOUGH_BORROW_ATOKEN_LIQUIDITY.

Impact: Core lending/borrowing market orders and liquidateWithReplacement are bricked on mainnet Aave (or the safety check is meaningless).

## [Medium] Compensate gives the lender a new credit position with forSale = true, overriding the lender's not-for-sale choice

File: Compensate.sol
Function: executeCompensate()

A lender can protect a credit position by setting forSale = false via setUserConfiguration. The borrower can call compensate(creditPositionWithDebtToRepay = lender's position, RESERVED_ID or own credit). The lender's position is reduced via reduceDebtAndCredit and the lender receives a credit position created through createDebtAndCreditPositions / createCreditPosition, which always initialise forSale = true (or inherit the borrower's forSale on full transfer). The lender's per-position opt-out is lost without consent.

Attack path: 1) Lender L, who also has a borrow offer, sets forSale=false on credit C1 against borrower B. 2) B calls compensate(C1, RESERVED_ID, amount). 3) L now owns a fresh credit C2 with forSale=true. 4) A third party calls buyCreditMarket(creditPositionId=C2) and buys L's credit at L's borrow curve, which L explicitly did not want.

Impact: Lenders' credit can be forcibly sold against their configuration; loss of expected yield / position.

## [Medium] getCreditAmountIn charges swap fee on net cash, under-collecting protocol fees and mispricing exactAmountIn=false sells

File: AccountingLibrary.sol
Function: getCreditAmountIn()

For sellCreditMarket with exactAmountIn=false the credit sold is creditAmountIn = (cashAmountOut + fragmentationFee) * (PERCENT + r) / (PERCENT - swapFeePercent), i.e. gross cash = (cash+frag)/(1-f). But fees are computed as mulDivUp(cashAmountOut, swapFeePercent, PERCENT) + fragmentationFee, on the net amount, so lender pays cash + cash*f + frag, less than the gross value (cash+frag)/(1-f) implied by the credit. The difference (frag*f + cash*f^2/(1-f)) is neither paid to the protocol nor consistent with the exactAmountIn=true path (getCashAmountOut), where the fee is on the gross amount.

Attack path: 1) Borrower calls sellCreditMarket with exactAmountIn=false. 2) Credit is sized as if the fee were taken on gross cash, but feeRecipient only receives fee on net cash. 3) Lender receives more credit than paid for at its own rate; protocol loses fees on every such trade.

Impact: Systematic protocol fee loss and inconsistent pricing between the two order modes (lender gets a rate better than its curve, borrower overpays).

## [Medium] Withdraw of borrow tokens is blocked when borrower CR is below opening limit even though cash is not collateral

File: Size.sol
Function: withdraw()

Size.withdraw always calls validateUserIsNotBelowOpeningLimitBorrowCR(msg.sender) after executeWithdraw, regardless of params.token. collateralRatio only uses collateralToken balance and debt; borrowAToken is not collateral. A user with debt whose CR dropped below crOpening (but above crLiquidation) cannot withdraw any borrowAToken, even though withdrawing it does not change their CR.

Attack path: 1) User has open debt and also lends/receives USDC (borrowAToken). 2) Price drops: CR is 140% with crOpening 150%. 3) User calls withdraw(USDC) and the call reverts with CR_BELOW_OPENING_LIMIT_BORROW_CR.

Impact: Users' cash (including borrowed funds or lending proceeds) is frozen in the protocol until they add collateral or repay.

## [Medium] Partial compensate with RESERVED_ID fragments credit without paying the fragmentation fee

File: Compensate.sol
Function: executeCompensate()

With creditPositionToCompensateId == RESERVED_ID and amount < creditPositionWithDebtToRepay.credit, createDebtAndCreditPositions creates a new debt and credit of amountToCompensate, the original credit is reduced, and createCreditPosition transfers the full new credit to the lender. The lender's single position is split into two (position count +1), yet exiterCreditRemaining = creditPositionToCompensate.credit - amountToCompensate = 0, so the fragmentationFee branch is skipped. The same split through sellCreditMarket/buyCreditMarket pays fragmentationFee.

Attack path: 1) Borrower B (or any user who is both lender and borrower) calls compensate(C1, RESERVED_ID, a) with a < C1.credit, repeatedly. 2) Each call creates new debt/credit positions with no fee.

Impact: Protocol loses fragmentation fees intended to pay for keeper/claim costs of extra positions; fee can be avoided systematically.

## [Medium] Liquidation remainder cap uses crLiquidation instead of (crLiquidation - 100%), over-charging overdue healthy borrowers

File: Liquidate.sol
Function: executeLiquidate()

collateralRemainderCap = mulDivDown(debtInCollateralToken, crLiquidation, PERCENT), and the protocol takes collateralProtocolPercent (overdue variant) of min(remainder, cap). The comment says the cap exists so that the split for non-underwater overdue loans is not too much. For an underwater loan the remainder is always below (crLiquidation - 1)*D, so the cap never binds; for an overdue loan at e.g. 300% CR the base becomes 1.3*D, larger than the full band any underwater loan could have. The intended cap appears to be the excess above debt, (crLiquidation - PERCENT)*D.

Attack path: 1) Healthy borrower (CR 300%) misses the due date. 2) Anyone liquidates; protocol receives overdueCollateralProtocolPercent of 1.3*D collateral instead of 0.3*D.

Impact: Borrowers of overdue but solvent loans lose more collateral to the fee recipient than intended.

## [Medium] collateralRatio and debt-to-collateral conversion assume 18-decimal collateral token

File: RiskLibrary.sol
Function: collateralRatio()

collateralRatio = collateral * price / debtWad, where debt is scaled to WAD with amountToWad but collateral (collateralToken.balanceOf) is used raw. Likewise debtTokenAmountToCollateralTokenAmount returns an 18-decimal amount and Liquidate compares it with raw collateral balances. Initialize accepts any underlyingCollateralToken with decimals <= 18 and the collateralToken mirrors those decimals.

Attack path: 1) Market deployed with an 8-decimal collateral (e.g. WBTC). 2) CR is computed 1e10 times too small, every borrower is instantly underwater and liquidatable; liquidation amounts in collateral are 1e10 too large.

Impact: Wrong solvency checks and liquidation amounts for any non-18-decimal collateral market.

## [Medium] Compensate requires borrower to not be underwater, preventing underwater borrowers from deleveraging

File: Size.sol
Function: compensate()

compensate ends with validateUserIsNotUnderwater(msg.sender). Compensating with an existing credit position reduces the borrower's debt (debtToken burned in repayDebt) without touching collateral, which can only improve CR. Yet a borrower who is underwater, or remains underwater after a partial compensation, gets reverted, so the only route to reduce debt is a full repay.

Attack path: 1) Borrower B is underwater on debt D1 and owns credit C on another healthy loan. 2) B calls compensate(D1-credit, C, amount) to cancel part of his debt. 3) CR improves but stays under crLiquidation, and the call reverts.

Impact: Borrowers cannot use their credit to rescue their position, leading to avoidable liquidations.

## [High] Liquidation reward uses futureValue (borrow-token units) but is added to collateral-token amounts

File: Liquidate.sol
Function: executeLiquidate()

In executeLiquidate the reward is Math.min(assignedCollateral - debtInCollateralToken, Math.mulDivUp(debtPosition.futureValue, feeConfig.liquidationRewardPercent, PERCENT)). assignedCollateral and debtInCollateralToken are in collateral-token units (WETH, 18 decimals), while debtPosition.futureValue is in borrow-token units (USDC, 6 decimals) and is never converted with debtTokenAmountToCollateralTokenAmount. liquidatorProfitCollateralToken = debtInCollateralToken + liquidatorReward therefore adds a number that is roughly 1e12 times too small for the supported WETH/USDC market (10,000 USDC at 5 percent gives 5e8 wei of WETH). The unit test Liquidate.t.sol recomputes the reward with the same futureValue formula, so it does not catch this. liquidateWithReplacement reuses the same code path through executeLiquidate.

Attack path: 1) A borrower becomes underwater (for example CR 125 percent with crLiquidation 130 percent) or an overdue loan exists. 2) A liquidator calls liquidate, pays futureValue in szaUSDC and receives collateral worth exactly the debt at the oracle price plus an almost zero reward. 3) After gas and oracle lag the liquidation is loss-making, so no rational liquidator acts. 4) Price keeps falling, the position drops below 100 percent CR and the lenders of that debt position can only self-liquidate for less than their credit.

Impact: The liquidation incentive that the protocol relies on is effectively zero, so underwater and overdue positions stay open and bad debt accrues to lenders.

## [Medium] Multicall cap check uses Size's own szaUSDC balance, so deposits inside multicall bypass borrowATokenCap

File: Multicall.sol
Function: multicall()

Deposit.executeDeposit skips validateBorrowATokenCap when state.data.isMulticall is true and relies on the check at the end of Multicall.multicall instead. That check reads borrowATokenSupplyBefore/After as state.data.borrowAToken.balanceOf(address(this)), which is the balance held by the Size contract itself (repaid or liquidated funds waiting for claim), not the total supply. A deposit mints szaUSDC to the depositor and leaves Size's own balance unchanged, so validateBorrowATokenIncreaseLteDebtTokenDecrease sees no increase (and its outer condition borrowATokenSupplyAfter > borrowATokenCap is practically never true). The cap check inside multicall is therefore vacuous.

Attack path: A user calls multicall with a single deposit(USDC, hugeAmount, to). isMulticall is true so validateBorrowATokenCap is skipped; at the end Size's own szaUSDC balance did not change, so no revert. The deposited amount can exceed riskConfig.borrowATokenCap by any size, and the same user can later withdraw.

Impact: The protocol-wide cap on deposited borrow tokens, a key risk-limiting parameter, can be bypassed by anyone with capital by wrapping a deposit in multicall.

## [High] Liquidity check reads the Aave Pool's underlying balance, but reserves sit in the aToken, so market orders revert

File: CapsLibrary.sol
Function: validateVariablePoolHasEnoughLiquidity()

validateVariablePoolHasEnoughLiquidity computes liquidity as state.data.underlyingBorrowToken.balanceOf(address(state.data.variablePool)). In Aave v3, supply() transfers the underlying to the reserve's aToken contract (the aTokenAddress that DepositTokenLibrary itself reads from getReserveData), not to the Pool, so the Pool holds essentially no USDC. The check is called at the end of buyCreditMarket, sellCreditMarket and liquidateWithReplacement. With a real Aave pool liquidity is about 0 and any positive amount reverts with NOT_ENOUGH_BORROW_ATOKEN_LIQUIDITY. The tests do not show this because test/mocks/PoolMock.sol pulls the underlying into the mock pool itself (transferFrom(msg.sender, address(this), amount)).

Attack path: No attacker needed: after deployment against real Aave v3, any user who calls buyCreditMarket or sellCreditMarket (or the keeper calling liquidateWithReplacement) with amount > 0 hits liquidity (about 0) < amount and the transaction reverts.

Impact: All market-order lending and borrowing and liquidateWithReplacement are bricked on mainnet/Base, or the check gives the wrong answer if some balance is donated to the Pool.

## [Medium] collateralRatio calls the oracle before checking debt, so withdrawals of debt-free users freeze on oracle outage

File: RiskLibrary.sol
Function: collateralRatio()

collateralRatio reads uint256 price = state.oracle.priceFeed.getPrice() unconditionally and only afterwards branches on if (debt != 0), returning type(uint256).max when the user has no debt. Size.withdraw always ends with validateUserIsNotBelowOpeningLimitBorrowCR(msg.sender), which calls collateralRatio. PriceFeed.getPrice reverts on a stale feed, an invalid price, SEQUENCER_DOWN, or during the one hour GRACE_PERIOD_TIME after a sequencer restart. So when the oracle is unavailable even users with zero debt (pure lenders and pure depositors) cannot withdraw either token.

Attack path: A Chainlink feed goes stale or the L2 sequencer is down/just restarted. Any user who has no debt calls withdraw for their USDC or WETH deposit; collateralRatio reverts inside getPrice, so the withdrawal reverts for as long as the outage plus grace period lasts.

Impact: Funds of users who never borrowed are frozen for the duration of an oracle or sequencer outage, although their withdrawal has no price-dependent risk.

## [Medium] Compensate with RESERVED_ID splits the lender's credit without fragmentation fee (fee gated on the wrong position)

File: Compensate.sol
Function: executeCompensate()

The fragmentation fee in executeCompensate is charged only if exiterCreditRemaining = creditPositionToCompensate.credit - amountToCompensate is greater than 0, i.e. when the compensating borrower's own credit is split. With creditPositionToCompensateId == RESERVED_ID the exiter is a freshly created position (createDebtAndCreditPositions with futureValue = amountToCompensate), so its remainder is always 0 and no fee is charged. In that path the position that really fractionalizes is the lender's: reduceDebtAndCredit lowers creditPositionWithDebtToRepay.credit to a non-zero remainder and createCreditPosition hands the lender a second position for amountToCompensate. This breaks FEES_01 (fee on every fractionalization). The new position also gets forSale = true from createDebtAndCreditPositions, regardless of the lender's flag.

Attack path: A borrower with a debt position owing lender L calls compensate with creditPositionWithDebtToRepayId = L's credit, creditPositionToCompensateId = RESERVED_ID and a small amount (at least minimumCreditBorrowAToken). L's single position becomes two positions (remainder plus a new one on a new debt position of the same borrower and due date), no fee is paid. The borrower repeats this to create many positions.

Impact: Fragmentation fees meant to fund claim costs are skipped, the borrower can multiply a lender's credit positions (more claim transactions and gas for the lender), and positions the lender had set not-for-sale come back as forSale.

## [Medium] Compensate swaps a lender's claim onto another borrower without the lender's consent

File: Compensate.sol
Function: validateCompensate()

In the existing-position path the borrower B of debt position D1 picks any credit position C_B (lender B) on a different debt position D2. validateCompensate only checks that D1 is ACTIVE, that D2 is transferrable (ACTIVE and its borrower not underwater, which means CR above crLiquidation rather than crOpening), that D1.dueDate >= D2.dueDate, that C_B.lender is B and that msg.sender is B. executeCompensate then reduces B's debt and the lender L's credit on D1 and gives L credit on D2 through createCreditPosition, without any check on L's allCreditPositionsForSaleDisabled, L's forSale flag or any opt-in. L's claim on B is replaced by a claim on borrower X of D2, and a newly created position for L is reset to forSale = true.

Attack path: B (or a colluding account) lends a small amount to an alt borrower X whose CR is just above crLiquidation, then calls compensate so that L's credit on B moves to a credit on X. B's debt falls and B can withdraw collateral down to the opening CR; X then goes underwater a moment later and L recovers less than from B.

Impact: Lenders can be involuntarily moved to a weaker counterparty (collateralised only to the liquidation threshold, not the opening threshold), shifting liquidation risk from borrowers onto lenders.

## [Medium] SellCreditMarket exactAmountIn=false charges a smaller swap fee than exactAmountIn=true for the same trade

File: AccountingLibrary.sol
Function: getCreditAmountIn()

For the same economic trade (borrower wants net cash C), the exactAmountIn=true path (getCashAmountOut) charges the swap fee on the gross present value X = F*PERCENT/(PERCENT+rate), so fees = s*X with X = C/(1-s), i.e. fees = C*s/(1-s), where s = swapFeePercent(tenor). The exactAmountIn=false path (getCreditAmountIn) charges fees = mulDivUp(cashAmountOut, s, PERCENT) = C*s on the net cash, then sets creditAmountIn = (C+fragFee)*(PERCENT+rate)/(PERCENT-s). The lender therefore pays C + C*s for credit that is worth C/(1-s) at the offered rate, and the fee recipient receives a factor (1-s) less than in the other mode. BuyCreditMarket applies the fee on the lender's gross cash in both modes, so the inconsistency is specific to the sell side.

Attack path: A borrower always passes exactAmountIn=false with the desired net cash instead of exactAmountIn=true with a credit amount and pays the lower fee. The shortfall is about notional * s^2 (for s = 0.5 percent that is 0.0025 percent of the cash, growing quadratically with fee and tenor).

Impact: Protocol swap fee revenue is slightly but systematically lower for takers who choose the cash-denominated mode, and the lender receives a marginally higher yield than quoted. Magnitude is small for realistic fee levels.

## [Medium] Liquidator reward cap mixes borrow-token units (futureValue) with collateral-token units, so the liquidation bonus is effectively zero

File: Liquidate.sol
Function: executeLiquidate()

In the profitable branch, liquidatorReward is Math.min(assignedCollateral - debtInCollateralToken, Math.mulDivUp(debtPosition.futureValue, feeConfig.liquidationRewardPercent, PERCENT)). debtPosition.futureValue is denominated in the borrow token (6 decimals for USDC), as debtTokenAmountToCollateralTokenAmount() shows when it converts that same value to collateral via the price feed. assignedCollateral and debtInCollateralToken are in collateral wei (18 decimals). The second argument of min() is therefore about 1e12 times smaller than the intended percentage of the debt value, so the min() returns that tiny number almost always. As a result liquidatorProfitCollateralToken collapses to debtInCollateralToken plus dust, and the remaining excess collateral goes to the protocol (collateralRemainder times collateralProtocolPercent) and to the borrower instead of the liquidator.

Attack path: A liquidator (liquidate() is public, KEEPER_ROLE is not required) calls liquidate({debtPositionId, minimumCollateralProfit}) on an ACTIVE or OVERDUE debt position whose borrower is underwater or whose loan is overdue, while the borrower's assigned collateral exceeds the debt value. The liquidator pays futureValue in borrow tokens and receives only the debt-equivalent collateral plus a dust reward. Its expected profit is about zero, so any non-zero minimumCollateralProfit makes the call revert, and bots have no reason to liquidate profitable positions.

Impact: The liquidation bonus that the fee config promises (liquidationRewardPercent of the debt value) is effectively never paid in profitable liquidations, and the excess collateral is split between protocol and borrower. Keepers are not compensated for liquidating, so underwater or overdue positions stay open longer, which raises the risk of bad debt for lenders.

## [Medium] Replacement liquidation sends the liquidator's borrow-token profit to feeRecipient instead of the caller

File: LiquidateWithReplacement.sol
Function: executeLiquidateWithReplacement()

executeLiquidateWithReplacement() computes liquidatorProfitBorrowToken = futureValue - issuanceValue and names it as the liquidator's profit, but the only transfer of that amount is borrowAToken.transferFrom(address(this), state.feeConfig.feeRecipient, liquidatorProfitBorrowToken). The caller (msg.sender, a KEEPER_ROLE holder) pays the full futureValue into the protocol inside executeLiquidate() via borrowAToken.transferFrom(msg.sender, address(this), futureValue), and then receives only the collateral profit. The new borrower receives issuanceValue, and the rate spread goes to the fee recipient. The code does not show whether this routing is intended, since no other path credits the spread to msg.sender.

Attack path: A KEEPER_ROLE address calls liquidateWithReplacement() on an ACTIVE debt position with a replacement borrower who has a valid borrowOffer. The keeper pays futureValue, the replacement borrower receives issuanceValue, and liquidatorProfitBorrowToken is sent to feeRecipient rather than to msg.sender.

Impact: The keeper pays the full face value of the debt and captures none of the rate spread, which weakens the incentive to use the replacement path. The spread is moved to the protocol fee recipient. Only trusted keeper accounts are affected, so the impact is economic and not permissionless.

## [Medium] Native ETH left in the contract by any payable call is swept by the next ETH deposit and credited to that depositor

File: Deposit.sol
Function: executeDeposit()

Every user-facing function is payable and no function refunds or tracks msg.value outside of deposit. ETH sent along with a non-deposit call, such as buyCreditLimit or repay with value, therefore stays in the Size proxy. When msg.value > 0, executeDeposit() sets amount = address(this).balance, wraps the whole balance into WETH with weth.deposit{value: amount}(), and mints the collateral deposit token to params.to. validateDeposit() only requires msg.value == params.amount, so an attacker can send a small msg.value equal to their chosen amount and the wrap sweeps the stranded balance as well. The README describes this sweep for ETH that is sent forcibly, but it does not mention ETH sent accidentally through other payable functions, and there is no native ETH withdrawal path.

Attack path: A user accidentally calls buyCreditLimit{value: 1 ether}(...), and the 1 ETH stays in the Size proxy. An attacker then calls deposit({token: WETH, amount: 1 wei, to: attacker}) with msg.value = 1 wei. executeDeposit wraps address(this).balance (1 ETH + 1 wei) and mints the full amount to the attacker.

Impact: ETH accidentally sent to any payable Size function is irrecoverable and goes to the next ETH depositor, who can take it with a dust-sized deposit. The loss falls on the user who sent the ETH by mistake.

## [High] Liquidator bonus cap mixes borrow-token units with collateral-token units, zeroing the liquidation reward

File: Liquidate.sol
Function: executeLiquidate()

In the profitable-liquidation branch the reward is capped as Math.mulDivUp(debtPosition.futureValue, state.feeConfig.liquidationRewardPercent, PERCENT). futureValue is denominated in underlyingBorrowToken base units, but the result is then used with Math.min against (assignedCollateral - debtInCollateralToken), which is denominated in collateral-token base units. The FeeConfig comment says the reward is a percent of futureValue, so the conversion to collateral units is missing. When the borrow token has fewer decimals than the collateral token (for example USDC debt against 18-decimal WETH collateral), the cap is about 10^12 times too small and the liquidator receives essentially only debtInCollateralToken. The excess collateral then stays with the borrower or is split to the protocol.

Attack path: A position becomes underwater (collateral ratio below crLiquidation) with its collateral worth more than the debt, for example WETH collateral against USDC debt. A liquidator calls liquidate(). executeLiquidate() computes liquidatorReward = min(excess, futureValue * liquidationRewardPercent / PERCENT) in mixed units, which is orders of magnitude below the intended percentage bonus. The liquidator pays futureValue and receives roughly the debt value in collateral with almost no bonus. Liquidations become break-even or unprofitable, so rational liquidators skip them.

Impact: The liquidation incentive is effectively removed for any pair whose collateral and borrow token decimals differ. Underwater and overdue positions go unliquidated, which increases bad-debt risk for lenders. The protocol and borrowers capture the excess that was meant for liquidators.

## [Medium] Multicall skips the borrow aToken cap and its end-of-batch check reads Size's own balance, not total supply

File: Multicall.sol
Function: multicall()

Deposit.executeDeposit() skips validateBorrowATokenCap() whenever state.data.isMulticall is true, relying on the end-of-batch check. That check, validateBorrowATokenIncreaseLteDebtTokenDecrease(), compares state.data.borrowAToken.balanceOf(address(this)), which is only Size's own position in its borrowAToken, against state.riskConfig.borrowATokenCap. The cap itself is defined on borrowAToken.totalSupply(), the sum of all user balances. A deposit with to set to a third party increases totalSupply through mintScaled(to, ...) but leaves Size's own balance unchanged. The batch-level check therefore never sees the deposit, and the cap is not enforced for deposits made inside a multicall.

Attack path: An attacker calls multicall() with a single deposit(borrowToken, largeAmount, to = attacker-controlled address) when totalSupply is at the cap. Inside the batch, no cap check runs. After the batch, Size's own borrowAToken balance has not increased, so the 'after > cap' branch is not taken and validateBorrowATokenIncreaseLteDebtTokenDecrease() passes. The resulting borrowAToken totalSupply is far above borrowATokenCap.

Impact: The governance-set borrowATokenCap, which limits how much of the variable pool the protocol can hold on behalf of depositors, can be exceeded at will. This exposes the protocol to liquidity and pool-risk limits the admin intended to enforce and can break the assumptions that the cap is supposed to protect.

## [Medium] deposit() credits the whole contract ETH balance, so ETH left in Size by other payable calls is stolen

File: Deposit.sol
Function: executeDeposit()

Every user action in Size is declared payable (repay, claim, buyCreditMarket, sellCreditMarket, withdraw, compensate, liquidate and others) but only deposit consumes msg.value. The contract has no receive() or fallback(), so ETH sent with those calls stays in the contract. When deposit() is called with msg.value > 0, executeDeposit() sets amount = address(this).balance instead of msg.value, wraps that full balance into WETH, and credits it to params.to. validateDeposit() only checks msg.value == params.amount, so the stuck ETH is credited to whoever makes the next deposit.

Attack path: A user calls repay{value: 1 ether}(...) by mistake, or includes ETH in a multicall that does not consume it. The 1 ETH stays in Size. An attacker then calls deposit({token: WETH, amount: 1 wei, to: attacker}) with msg.value = 1 wei. validateDeposit passes because msg.value equals params.amount. executeDeposit wraps the full contract balance, 1 ETH + 1 wei, and mints that amount of collateral to the attacker.

Impact: ETH accidentally sent to any payable Size function can be taken by the next caller of deposit() for almost no cost. The loss is the stuck balance of the victim, and an attacker can also drain any ETH that accumulates in the contract.

## [Medium] LiquidateWithReplacement sends the keeper's borrow-token profit to the fee recipient instead of the liquidator

File: LiquidateWithReplacement.sol
Function: executeLiquidateWithReplacement()

The function computes liquidatorProfitBorrowToken = futureValue - issuanceValue and names it a liquidator profit. It returns that value to Size.liquidateWithReplacement() as the liquidator's profit. The actual transfer, however, sends it to state.feeConfig.feeRecipient: state.data.borrowAToken.transferFrom(address(this), state.feeConfig.feeRecipient, liquidatorProfitBorrowToken). The keeper pays the full futureValue into Size through executeLiquidate(), and only the collateral profit reaches the keeper. Whether the discount was meant for the keeper or the protocol is not clear from the code.

Attack path: A KEEPER_ROLE holder calls liquidateWithReplacement() on an underwater ACTIVE loan and selects a replacement borrower with a borrow offer. executeLiquidate() takes futureValue from the keeper. Size then pays issuanceValue to the replacement borrower and sends the remainder (futureValue - issuanceValue) to feeRecipient, while the function reports that remainder as the liquidator's profit. The keeper's economics diverge from what the return value advertises.

Impact: The keeper bears the cost of the discount routing and cannot capture it, so the replacement-liquidation path is less attractive to keepers. If the naming reflects the intent, the discount is misrouted to the protocol treasury.

## [Medium] Collateral ratio and collateral-value conversion assume 18-decimal collateral, but deployment allows fewer decimals

File: RiskLibrary.sol
Function: collateralRatio()

collateralRatio() computes mulDivDown(collateral, price, debtWad), where collateral is the raw collateralToken balance and debtWad is the debt scaled to 18 decimals. This is only correct if the collateral token has 18 decimals. debtTokenAmountToCollateralTokenAmount() in AccountingLibrary has the same assumption: it returns an 18-decimal amount that is used directly as a collateral-token amount, in Liquidate and Compensate. Initialize.validateInitializeDataParams() only requires underlyingCollateralToken decimals <= 18, so a 6-decimal collateral such as USDC is accepted at deployment. For such a token the computed collateral ratio is about 10^12 times too small.

Attack path: The admin deploys Size with a collateral token that has fewer than 18 decimals and passes initialize(). For any borrower with debt, collateralRatio() returns a value far below crLiquidation. isUserUnderwater() becomes true for everyone with debt, so anyone can call liquidate() or selfLiquidate() on healthy positions, and new borrows are blocked by the crOpening check.

Impact: With a non-18-decimal collateral token, every borrower is treated as underwater and healthy loans become liquidatable, or the protocol becomes unusable. This is a silent configuration failure, since initialize() accepts the token.

## [High] Liquidator reward cap compares borrow-token futureValue against a collateral-token surplus

File: Liquidate.sol
Function: executeLiquidate()

In the profitable-liquidation branch, liquidatorReward is Math.min(assignedCollateral - debtInCollateralToken, Math.mulDivUp(debtPosition.futureValue, liquidationRewardPercent, PERCENT)). The first operand is in collateral-token units because assignedCollateral is the borrower's collateral and debtInCollateralToken comes from debtTokenAmountToCollateralTokenAmount. The second operand multiplies futureValue, which is in borrow-token units (debtToken and borrowAToken amounts). When the two tokens differ in decimals or price, the cap has the wrong magnitude and min() almost always picks the wrong operand. The cap should be a percentage of debtInCollateralToken, which is already the debt expressed in collateral units.

Attack path: The protocol uses an 18-decimal collateral token and a 6-decimal borrow token. An overdue or underwater borrower has collateral worth more than the debt. A liquidator calls liquidate(debtPositionId) on a debt of 1000 USDC, where futureValue is 1e9 units. The 1 percent reward cap becomes 1e7 wei of collateral, roughly 1e-11 ETH, instead of about 1 percent of the debt value. The liquidator therefore receives only the debt value in collateral, pays the debt in USDC, and nets roughly zero.

Impact: For most token pairs the liquidator reward is close to zero, so the liquidationRewardPercent incentive does not work. Underwater or overdue loans with surplus collateral become unprofitable to liquidate and can stay open until they turn into bad debt for lenders. The error does not show up in tests that use equal decimals and a 1:1 price.

## [Medium] Deposit credits the whole ETH balance of Size, so stray ETH goes to the next ETH depositor

File: Deposit.sol
Function: executeDeposit()

When msg.value > 0, executeDeposit sets amount = address(this).balance instead of params.amount, wraps that full balance into WETH and credits the resulting token amount to params.to. validateDeposit only checks that msg.value equals params.amount, so it does not bound the contract balance. Many entry points are payable (deposit, withdraw, buyCreditMarket, sellCreditMarket, repay, compensate, liquidate and others), none of them refunds or sweeps ETH, and ETH can also be forced in with selfdestruct. Any ETH already held by the contract is therefore minted as a deposit for the next caller who deposits ETH.

Attack path: A user accidentally sends 1 ETH to a payable Size function such as repay(), and the ETH stays in the contract. An attacker then calls deposit with token equal to WETH (the underlying borrow token), amount 1 ETH, to set to the attacker, and msg.value 1 ETH. validateDeposit passes because msg.value equals amount. executeDeposit then uses amount = address(this).balance = 2 ETH, so the attacker receives 2 units of the Size token for 1 ETH paid.

Impact: ETH that was sent to the protocol by mistake, or forced in, can be claimed by any later depositor. The victim loses the stray ETH and the attacker gets an inflated position. No protocol insolvency follows directly, but the value transfer is unintended theft from the sender.

## [Medium] Collateral ratio assumes 18-decimal collateral, but Initialize accepts collateral with fewer decimals

File: RiskLibrary.sol
Function: collateralRatio()

collateralRatio reads collateral = collateralToken.balanceOf(account) in the collateral token's native decimals and multiplies it by the 18-decimal price. Only the debt side is normalised with Math.amountToWad. AccountingLibrary.debtTokenAmountToCollateralTokenAmount likewise returns a collateral amount computed as if the collateral had 18 decimals. Initialize.validateInitializeDataParams only requires underlyingCollateralToken.decimals() <= 18, so a 6-decimal collateral token passes validation. Every collateral ratio and debt-to-collateral conversion is then about 10^12 times too small.

Attack path: The admin deploys Size with a 6-decimal collateral token, which validation accepts. For a borrower with 1 token of collateral and 1000 units of debt at price 3000, the ratio is computed as about 3e-12 instead of 3. isUserUnderwater is therefore true for every borrower with debt, and isDebtPositionLiquidatable returns true. Anyone can liquidate healthy borrowers and take their collateral for the debt value.

Impact: Mass false liquidations of healthy positions, plus wrong collateral amounts in liquidations and fragmentation-fee charges, if a non-18-decimal collateral token is configured. The root cause is a validation gap that lets a deployment choice break the core math, so the severity depends on the admin configuration.

## [Medium] Multicall cap check measures Size's own aToken balance, so deposits to users bypass borrowATokenCap

File: Multicall.sol
Function: multicall()

multicall records borrowATokenSupplyBefore and borrowATokenSupplyAfter with borrowAToken.balanceOf(address(this)), which is the unscaled balance held by the Size contract itself, not borrowAToken.totalSupply(). The values are passed to validateBorrowATokenIncreaseLteDebtTokenDecrease, which checks the cap. Deposit.executeDeposit skips validateBorrowATokenCap while isMulticall is true, relying on the end-of-batch check instead. A deposit whose recipient is another user mints borrowAToken to that user and leaves Size's own balance unchanged, so the measured increase is zero and the cap check never fires.

Attack path: The borrowAToken supply is at the cap. An attacker calls multicall with one element: deposit(token = underlyingBorrowToken, amount = X, to = attacker). Inside the batch, isMulticall is true so the per-deposit cap check is skipped, and borrowAToken is minted to the attacker. After the batch, Size's balanceOf is unchanged, so borrowATokenSupplyAfter minus borrowATokenSupplyBefore is 0 and the check passes. borrowAToken.totalSupply() ends above borrowATokenCap by X, and the same bypass can be repeated.

Impact: The protocol's borrow-side supply cap, which is a risk limit on how much cash can be lent into the variable pool, can be exceeded without bound through multicall. The check that is meant to protect the cap is ineffective for exactly the deposit path it replaces.

## [Medium] Sell with exact cash out charges a smaller swap fee than the exact credit-in path for the same trade

File: AccountingLibrary.sol
Function: getCreditAmountIn()

On the exact-cash-out path, getCreditAmountIn charges fees = mulDivUp(cashAmountOut, swapFeePercent, PERCENT) on the net cash the borrower receives, and the lender pays cashAmountOut plus that fee. On the exact-credit-in path, getCashAmountOut charges the fee on maxCashAmountOut, the gross cash = creditIn * PERCENT / (PERCENT + ratePerTenor), and the seller receives maxCashAmountOut minus that fee. For the same credit amount, the gross cash implied by the exact-cash-out formula is cashOut * PERCENT / (PERCENT - swapFeePercent), not cashOut plus cashOut * swapFeePercent / PERCENT. So the exact-cash-out path collects the swap fee on cashOut instead of on the gross cash, which under-collects the fee by a fraction of about swapFeePercent / PERCENT relative to the exact-credit-in path, and the lender pays less per unit of credit than on that path.

Attack path: A lender compares two ways to buy the same credit from the same borrowOffer. With swapFeePercent = 1e16 (1 percent), an exact-credit-in trade on credit C gives the seller maxCash*(1 - 0.01) and the protocol maxCash*0.01, with maxCash = C*PERCENT/(PERCENT+rate). The equivalent exact-cash-out trade on the same credit (cashOut = maxCash*0.99) charges the fee as 0.01 * cashOut, which is about 1 percent less of the protocol fee. The lender therefore pays a lower total for the same credit by using the exact-cash-out flag.

Impact: The protocol fee depends on which flag the taker uses, and it is about one percent lower on the exact-cash-out path at a 1 percent swap fee. The effect is small and scales with swapFeeAPR times tenor, so the magnitude is limited, but the same trade has two different prices.
