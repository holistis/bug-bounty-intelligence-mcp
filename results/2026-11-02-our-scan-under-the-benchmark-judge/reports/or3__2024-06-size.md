# Audit report

## [High] Liquidator reward computed from futureValue in borrow-token units but added to collateral-token amounts

File: Liquidate.sol
Function: executeLiquidate()

In the profitable branch, liquidatorReward = min(assignedCollateral - debtInCollateralToken, mulDivUp(debtPosition.futureValue, liquidationRewardPercent, PERCENT)). The first operand is in collateral-token units (e.g. 18 decimals). The second is futureValue times a percent, which is in borrow-token units (e.g. 6 decimals for USDC). The min is taken across two different units, and the result is added to debtInCollateralToken as if it were collateral. The reward should be derived from debtInCollateralToken or assignedCollateral, or be converted with debtTokenAmountToCollateralTokenAmount.

Attack path: 1. A position becomes liquidatable, either underwater or overdue, with borrow token at 6 decimals and collateral at 18 decimals. 2. A liquidator calls liquidate() and pays futureValue. 3. The reward comes out about 1e12 times too small, so the liquidator receives roughly debtInCollateralToken (zero margin) before gas. 4. No one has a reason to liquidate. Instead, collateralRemainder is larger, so the protocol fee and borrower remainder take what should have been the reward.

Impact: Liquidations are not incentivized when the two tokens have different decimals. Underwater positions can go unliquidated, which accrues bad debt for lenders. Protocol solvency depends on the liquidation incentive working.

## [Medium] Borrow aToken cap is bypassable inside multicall because the check measures the Size contract's own balance

File: Multicall.sol
Function: multicall()

deposit() skips validateBorrowATokenCap when state.data.isMulticall is true, relying on the end-of-multicall check. That check uses borrowAToken.balanceOf(address(this)) before and after, which is only the Size contract's own escrow of aTokens (repayment and liquidation proceeds). It is not the total supply. A borrow-token deposit mints to params.to, usually the user, so address(this)'s balance does not change. The cap condition 'supplyAfter > cap' and the increase-versus-debt-decrease comparison are therefore evaluated on the wrong quantity, and a deposit-only multicall passes. The check should use totalSupply().

Attack path: 1. The borrowATokenCap is reached. 2. A user calls multicall([deposit(borrowToken, amount, self)]), possibly with a no-op second call. 3. deposit() sees isMulticall == true and skips the cap. 4. The final check sees no change in Size's own balance and passes. 5. Deposits are credited beyond the cap.

Impact: The risk cap on deposited borrow tokens, a core launch safeguard, can be exceeded by anyone through multicall.

## [Medium] collateralRatio always queries the oracle, even when the user has no debt

File: RiskLibrary.sol
Function: collateralRatio()

collateralRatio() calls state.oracle.priceFeed.getPrice() unconditionally, before checking debt != 0. getPrice() reverts when the sequencer is down, during the grace period, or when a feed is stale or invalid. validateUserIsNotBelowOpeningLimitBorrowCR is executed on every withdraw(), including withdrawals of borrow tokens by pure lenders and of collateral by users with zero debt. Those users do not need the price at all.

Attack path: 1. A Chainlink feed goes stale or the L2 sequencer restarts. 2. Any user calls withdraw() for their own cash or collateral. 3. The call reverts in getPrice() even though the user has no debt.

Impact: All withdrawals, including lenders withdrawing cash and debt-free users withdrawing collateral, are frozen during oracle outages.

## [Medium] Collateral amount is not normalized to 18 decimals in CR and collateral conversion

File: RiskLibrary.sol
Function: collateralRatio()

collateralRatio() computes collateral * price / debtWad. debtWad is converted to 18 decimals, but the collateral balance is used raw. debtTokenAmountToCollateralTokenAmount() likewise returns a wad-scaled number that is compared and transferred as collateral-token units. validateInitializeDataParams allows collateral tokens with decimals below 18. With such a token (e.g. 8 decimals) the ratio is understated by 1e(18-decimals), and liquidation amounts are scaled wrongly.

Attack path: 1. The market is deployed with a collateral token that has fewer than 18 decimals. 2. Every borrower looks massively underwater, or conversions are inflated. 3. Liquidations and openings behave incorrectly.

Impact: Mis-liquidation or a broken market for non-18-decimal collateral that initialization accepts.
